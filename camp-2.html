Results

What We Found

A1C shows average blood sugar over the past 2–3 months. A screening result is not a diagnosis, so we report ranges. A doctor needs to confirm any high result.

In all, 69 of the 74 people tested (93%) had an A1C in the prediabetes or diabetes range, and 35 (47%) were above 8.0%, well into the diabetes range. Most of the people tested were men in their 50s and 60s.

Follow-up

What Happened Next

Reflection

Lessons Learned

  • Ask a few more questions at sign-up

    At Camp 2 we recorded each person's age, gender and A1C result, but not whether they had been tested before or already knew they had diabetes. That made it hard to tell how many people learned something new. At future camps, everyone will be invited to fill in a short, optional survey: height and weight, whether they've had an A1C test before, whether a doctor has ever told them they have diabetes or prediabetes, and whether they've noticed common signs such as unusual thirst, needing to urinate often, or feeling tired all the time.

  • The survey is for understanding, not advice

    The survey helps us see patterns across the community, such as how many people are being tested for the first time. It isn't a medical check, and we don't give health advice based on the answers. We only ever share totals, never anyone's individual answers. Anyone with a high A1C result should see a doctor.
